# Decisions

Every non-obvious call made while building this, and why. Numbered so code
comments can point at them.

---

## D-001 · Coverage is a three-way split, not known/unknown

**Decision.** Every 汉字 in a piece of content is classified as **known**,
**target** (a declared teaching word: carded, glossed, voiced, introduced in
context) or **incidental** (unknown and undeclared). Only incidental characters
block content. Proper nouns are removed from the denominator entirely.

**Why.** The two-way reading makes the brief's own numbers impossible. Serving at
"90–95% known coverage" *and* "introducing 5–10 target words" cannot both hold if
targets count as unknown — 5–10 new words in a 250-character chapter is already
5% of the text, so a 95% floor rejects every chapter that teaches anything.

This was not theoretical. The first machine check of the seed chapters
(`scripts/check-coverage.ts`) rejected chapter 2 because 伞 appeared 13 times —
5.4% of tokens — even though 伞 was the chapter's headline target word, complete
with a card and a gloss. Chapter 3 was penalised 12% of its coverage budget for
the three band-4 characters in 林阿姨, the name of its own recurring character.

**Consequence.** `splitCoverage` returns all four buckets; `verifyCoverage`
checks them in order of how badly each breaks a reader (incidental → too-hard →
target budget → too-easy). Chapter scripts declare `properNouns`.

## D-002 · libSQL/SQLite over Postgres or D1

**Decision.** Local SQLite file via `@libsql/client` + Drizzle, not Supabase or
Cloudflare D1 as the brief suggested.

**Why.** The definition of done says "the app runs locally with one command".
Supabase means an account, a project and a connection string; D1 means Wrangler
and a Cloudflare login. libSQL means a file. There is no third party holding a
13-year-old's voice recordings and error history, which also satisfies the
privacy constraint by construction rather than by policy.

**Migration path.** `DATABASE_URL` already accepts a `libsql://` URL. Pointing it
at Turso and adding `DATABASE_AUTH_TOKEN` is the whole change — the Drizzle
dialect is identical.

## D-003 · Hand-written DDL, guarded by a drift test

**Decision.** `src/lib/db/ddl.ts` duplicates the Drizzle schema as raw
`CREATE TABLE` statements. No drizzle-kit, no migration files.

**Why.** `npm run dev` has to be one command that works on a clean checkout.
Adding a migration step is one more thing to go wrong on the machine of someone
who just wants to see their son use the app.

**The catch, and the guard.** Duplication drifts. `tests/infra.test.ts` walks the
Drizzle schema with `getTableConfig` and asserts that every table, every column
and every index has a match in the DDL — and that the DDL declares nothing the
schema does not. Forgetting to update one side fails the suite.

## D-004 · Pinyin is resolved against our own word list, not pinyin-pro's segmenter

**Decision.** `segmentReadings()` does longest-match segmentation against the
9,443-word HSK lexicon and takes each word's stored reading. pinyin-pro is the
fallback for characters no word covers.

**Why.** This was found by a test, not by reasoning. pinyin-pro renders the
isolated word 行走 correctly as `xíng zǒu`, but inside the sentence 他在行走 its
segmenter returns `tā zài háng zǒu` — the wrong reading of a 多音字. 574 of the
3,000 HSK characters (**19%**) have more than one reading, and the reader is a
beginner who cannot spot the error. A wrong reading is worse than no reading.

The word-level readings in `lexicon.json` are themselves generated by pinyin-pro
at build time on *isolated* words, where it is reliable.

## D-005 · The coverage gate is a ramp, not a wall

**Decision.** `gateChapter` has three outcomes: `serve`, `pre-teach` (a bounded
set of characters, max 12, taught first as a "gear up" screen) and `reject`.

**Why.** "Verify coverage before a chapter is served" read literally means
refusing chapters that fail — and at the start *every* chapter fails, so the
player gets an empty campaign on day one. Pre-teaching keeps the guarantee that
no chapter is ever *read* below its floor, while still letting the story start.

Greedy selection is optimal for picking what to pre-teach: each character
contributes exactly its own token count and they are independent, so
biggest-first is the shortest path to the floor.

## D-006 · "Too easy" is a warning for authored chapters, a hard failure for generated ones

**Decision.** `verifyCoverage` fails content above the 97% known ceiling.
`gateChapter` downgrades that specific failure to a warning and serves anyway.

**Why.** A seed chapter sits at a fixed point in a story. Refusing to tell
chapter one because the player already knows its words breaks the narrative to
enforce a content-quality rule. For *generated* chapters the alternative is
simply "write a better chapter", so there the ceiling stays hard.

## D-007 · 应用文 is marked by rule; only 内容 and 语言 go to an LLM

**Decision.** `checkPracticalFormat` structurally validates 公函, 通告 and 启事
against the layout specified in the 考试纲要 appendix — date line, `致：` opener,
underlined title with no full stop, numbered middle paragraphs, three-line
signature block, bracketed printed name.

**Why.** The 纲要 specifies these formats down to the line. Those are checkable
rules, and a rule check is deterministic, instant, free and *correct* — three
properties an LLM does not have. Format is 40% of the 应用文 mark, so this is
most of the score available without any API key.

## D-008 · Option ids are positional in the client payload

**Decision.** `publicItem` re-keys every option to `o0..oN` and `resolveOption`
maps back on submit.

**Why.** Found by a test. The authored item bank used `a/b/c/d` with the key
always `'a'`; the generated items used `k` for the key and `d0/d1/d2` for
distractors. Both conventions shipped the key to the browser — `id: "k"` is a
large arrow pointing at the right answer, and `'a'` is worse. A 13-year-old will
find that, because finding it is more interesting than the homework. Position
leaks nothing: generated items shuffle before serialisation.

## D-009 · The baseline item is pinned in run state

**Decision.** `ensureItem(state)` generates an item once, writes it into
`state.served[responses.length]`, and every route reads it from there.

**Why.** A real bug caught in end-to-end testing. Item generation draws randomly
from the lexicon, so `/api/baseline/start` served 转 and `/api/baseline/answer`
regenerated a *different* character (母) and marked the student's answer against
that. Pinning also makes a mid-question page refresh safe, which is a precondition
for the brief's "splittable into 2 sittings".

## D-010 · Adaptive staircase for recognition only

**Decision.** Character recognition uses a 2-down-1-up staircase with a
reversal-average readout. The other seven skills are short fixed-form blocks.

**Why.** A staircase converges on roughly the 70%-correct point, which is where a
proficiency estimate carries most information, and it gets there in far fewer
items than a fixed form — which matters when 40 minutes has to cover eight
skills. But it needs enough items to produce reversals. Over six items it would
mostly measure noise, so the other skills get fixed blocks calibrated off the
recognition estimate.

The reversal average is used rather than "the band you finished at", which is
hostage to the last answer.

## D-011 · Skill weights are deliberately unequal

**Decision.** `recognition` 0.22, `comprehension` 0.17, `vocabulary` 0.13,
`languageKnowledge` 0.12, `pinyinTone`/`readAloud`/`writing` 0.10, `handwriting`
0.06.

**Why.** Recognition is the binding constraint — every other skill is capped by
it (docs/research.md §4.3). Comprehension and writing follow because between them
they are 60% of the 统考 paper. Handwriting is lowest: it costs 错别字 marks but
never blocks progress through the campaign.

## D-012 · Front-load 古诗文 and 语文基础知识

**Decision.** The relic system and the 语文基础知识 drills unlock early, ahead of
where a pure difficulty ordering would put them.

**Why.** Straight from the gap analysis: these are the two areas where an
English-dominant student is *not* behind, because 文言文 and grammar terminology
are new to every 初一 student. Both are explicitly examinable. They produce
visible exam gains while the slow 识字 curve is still climbing — which matters
for motivation as much as for marks.

## D-013 · The 14 relics are a closed set, shipped complete

**Decision.** All 14 poems ship in the repo with full text, line-by-line English,
文言实词 notes and 多音字 traps.

**Why.** The 考试纲要 names exactly which poems are 默写-examinable, with 册别 and
课次. It is a closed, known list, and every one is public domain (all authors died
well over a century ago). Verified line by line against zh.wikisource.org and
converted to simplified. This is the one piece of exam-critical content that can
be shipped complete rather than generated or uploaded.

## D-014 · Codex is invoked through its Node entry point, never a shell

**Decision.** `resolveCodex()` finds
`<npm prefix>/node_modules/@openai/codex/bin/codex.js` and runs it with
`execFile(process.execPath, [codexJs, ...args])`.

**Why.** On Windows the `codex` on PATH is a `.cmd` shim, which `execFile` cannot
run. The obvious fix — `shell: true` — would mean interpolating a
multi-paragraph art prompt into a command string. Resolving the real Node script
keeps arguments an array on every platform.

**Also learned the hard way:** do *not* pass
`-c sandbox_workspace_write.writable_roots=[...]`. Codex stages generated images
under `~/.codex/generated_images` before copying them to the target, so narrowing
the writable roots to `art/out` silently breaks generation — the model produces
the image and then cannot save it anywhere.

## D-015 · The art pipeline never trusts Codex's report

**Decision.** After every `codex exec`, `gen-art.ts` independently stats the
target path and checks the PNG magic bytes. Two attempts, then a visible
placeholder and `status: "failed"` in the manifest.

**Why.** `codex exec` returns prose. It will cheerfully say "Saved to …" when it
did not. The probe run confirmed the mechanism: Codex generates into its own
staging directory and then shells out to copy the file, so there are two places
it can fail while still reporting success.

## D-016 · No text in any generated image, ever

**Decision.** Every assembled prompt carries an explicit no-text clause, and the
style guide requires surfaces that would normally carry writing to be blank,
abstracted or turned away. All UI text is overlaid by React from the database.

**Why.** Generative models render Chinese characters as convincing-looking
nonsense. In a Chinese-learning app that is worse than useless — the student
would read a malformed character and learn it wrong.

## D-017 · Dev watcher excludes the data directory

**Decision.** `next.config.mjs` sets `watchOptions.ignored` for `data/`,
`art/out/`, `public/art/` and `public/audio/`.

**Why.** A real, reproducible bug. The SQLite file lives in `./data` and is
written on essentially every request. Each write tripped Next's dev file watcher,
which recompiled mid-request and failed the in-flight request with a bare
`SyntaxError: Unexpected end of JSON input` from its own module loader —
reproducibly, on the third answer of every baseline run. Production was
unaffected, which is what made it confusing.

## D-018 · Every route parses its body inside the error handler

**Decision.** `body(req)` is called inside `route(async () => …)`, never at the
top of the handler.

**Why.** `await req.json()` at the top of a handler puts the parse outside the
try/catch, so a malformed or empty body escapes as an unhandled `SyntaxError` and
Next renders its HTML error page. A fetch client then fails to parse *that*,
reporting an error two layers away from the real one. Every route was refactored.

## D-019 · Queue is capped and interleaved

**Decision.** `pickReviewQueue` never hands back the full due set. It caps the
session, interleaves new cards rather than appending them, and reports
`deferred` so the UI can say "and N more tomorrow".

**Why.** A 13-year-old who opens the app to a 180-card backlog stops opening the
app. Overdue cards are not lost — they wait. Appending new cards would end every
session with a wall of unfamiliar material at exactly the point attention is
lowest.

## D-020 · Failure is never punished

**Decision.** No streak penalty, no lives, no lost XP, no pay-to-win. A wrong
comprehension choice routes to a `detour` node that costs time and re-teaches the
misread word, then rejoins the spine. `validateScript` enforces that every detour
names where it rejoins, and that no node is unreachable.

**Why.** The brief asks for it, and docs/research.md §5.3 gives the reason:
loss-framed mechanics suppress voluntary practice in adolescents, which is the
opposite of what a daily-habit app needs.

## D-021 · Card level is driven by FSRS stability

**Decision.** Card level 1–5 comes from stability thresholds (0/1/7/21/60 days),
not from repetition count.

**Why.** Stability is the model's own estimate of how long the memory will hold,
which is what "mastery" means. Ten reviews of a card you keep forgetting is not
mastery, and rewarding it with a visible level-up would teach the wrong lesson.

## D-022 · Support level is computed per chapter, not set globally

**Decision.** `supportLevel()` derives `full` / `pinyin` / `none` from the
player's coverage of *that chapter*.

**Why.** The brief asks for English to fade as mastery grows. A global setting
would strand the player the first time a harder chapter arrives; a per-chapter
computation brings the training wheels back automatically exactly when they are
needed.

## D-023 · Names are never quizzed

**Decision.** `properNouns` characters are excluded from coverage and never
become cards or items. They are glossed permanently in the cast list.

**Why.** 林 阿 姨 are band-4 characters that happen to spell a character's name.
Charging a chapter a tenth of its coverage budget for naming its own cast
measures nothing useful, and drilling them as vocabulary teaches nothing about
Chinese.

## D-024 · Two local accounts, no identity provider

**Decision.** scrypt-hashed passwords in the local database, httpOnly session
cookie, no email, no OAuth, no analytics of any kind.

**Why.** This is a household app holding a child's voice recordings and error
history. The easiest way to honour "child data and voice recordings stay private"
is to have nothing that could leak it. Seeded passwords are printed once and
meant to be changed.

## D-025 · Recordings are stored as files, with a traversal guard

**Decision.** Audio goes to `data/recordings/`; only the path is stored. The
playback route resolves the path against that directory and refuses anything
that escapes it.

**Why.** The stored path comes from our own insert, but a stored value is still
input, and a child's audio directory is not where you want to find out you were
wrong about that.
